Production builders install a kitchen that's fast to build and easy to standardize across a whole subdivision — laminate counters, stock cabinets, and a layout chosen for construction speed rather than how you actually cook. A kitchen remodel is consistently the highest-ROI project tract-home owners take on. Here's what it actually costs.

## Three Budget Tiers

**Cosmetic refresh — under $15,000.** New paint, hardware, countertops, and a backsplash without touching the layout or cabinet boxes. The fastest way to update a builder-grade kitchen without a full renovation timeline.

**Mid-range remodel — $25,000–$60,000.** New cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, and lighting, with the plumbing and electrical staying roughly where they are. This is where most tract-home kitchen remodels land, and $30,000 is genuinely enough for a solid mid-grade remodel as long as the layout isn't changing.

**Full renovation with layout changes — $60,000+.** Moving plumbing, removing a wall for an open-concept layout, or relocating the island pushes the number up quickly, but it's also where you get the biggest functional upgrade over the original builder floor plan.

## Why the Layout Question Matters Most

The single biggest cost lever in any kitchen remodel isn't the cabinet grade or the countertop material — it's whether plumbing and walls move. If your existing layout works and you just want better materials and finishes, you can get a genuinely high-end look while staying in the mid-range tier. If you're opening the kitchen to the living room or relocating the sink and island, budget for the higher tier and the permitting that comes with it.

## The Right Order of Operations

A remodel that goes out of sequence is where budgets and timelines both blow up. The order that avoids rework:

- Demo
- Rough plumbing and electrical
- Drywall
- Flooring
- Cabinets
- Countertops
- Backsplash
- Appliances
- Final punch list

## Production Floor Plans Have a Pattern

Because tract homes repeat the same handful of floor plans across a subdivision, we've usually already remodeled a kitchen with your exact layout, or something very close to it. That means we can tell you early — before you commit to a budget — whether an open-concept conversion is a simple non-load-bearing wall removal or a bigger structural project, instead of finding out mid-demo.
