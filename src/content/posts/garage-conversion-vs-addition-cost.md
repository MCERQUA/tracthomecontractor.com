If you need more living space and you're weighing a garage conversion against a home addition, the cost gap is bigger than most homeowners expect — and it comes down to one simple fact: a garage conversion reuses a structure, foundation, and roof that already exist.

## The Cost Comparison

**Garage conversion: $15,000–$40,000.** The exact number depends on whether you're adding plumbing (a bathroom or kitchenette), insulation, and HVAC extension. Because the shell of the building is already there, you're paying almost entirely for finish-out, not new construction.

**Home addition: $70,000–$350,000+.** Even a modest 400 sq ft single-story addition runs $70,000–$160,000, because you're building a new foundation, framing, roofline tie-in, and full systems extension from scratch.

**The takeaway:** garage conversions typically run **30–50% cheaper** than a comparable addition for similar usable square footage.

## What You Give Up With a Garage Conversion

The math favors the garage conversion, but it's not free of tradeoffs:

- **You lose covered parking**, which matters for resale in some markets and for day-to-day convenience in almost all of them.
- **Ceiling height and window placement** are constrained by the existing structure — a garage wasn't designed as living space, so egress windows and insulation retrofits are part of nearly every conversion.
- **Permitting is still required.** Nearly every jurisdiction requires a building permit for a garage-to-living-space conversion, especially once plumbing or a bedroom-code egress window is involved. Skipping this step creates real problems at resale.

## When an Addition Makes More Sense

If you still need the garage for parking or storage, or if your lot has room and you want a true accessory dwelling unit (ADU) for rental income or a private in-law suite, a detached addition or standalone ADU is the better fit — even at the higher cost. It's also the right call if the space you need is larger than your garage footprint can support.

## Is a Bathroom in the Garage Legal?

Yes — when it's permitted and inspected. Adding a bathroom to a garage conversion requires proper venting, a code-compliant water supply, and a sewer or septic tie-in, all of which we handle as part of the permitting process on these projects.
