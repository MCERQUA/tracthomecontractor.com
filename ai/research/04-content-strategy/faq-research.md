# FAQ Research — real PAA + niche questions, source: DataForSEO PAA pulls 2026-08-20

## Kitchen Remodeling
1. What is a realistic budget for a kitchen remodel? — Mid-range full kitchen remodels typically run $25,000-$60,000 depending on layout changes, cabinetry, and countertop material; cosmetic refreshes (paint, hardware, counters only) can land under $15,000.
2. Is $30,000 enough for a kitchen remodel? — Yes, for a mid-grade remodel with new cabinets, countertops, and appliances if the layout stays the same; moving plumbing or walls pushes the number up.
3. In what order should a remodel be done? — Demo, rough plumbing/electrical, drywall, flooring, cabinets, countertops, backsplash, appliances, final punch list.

## Bathroom Remodeling
4. Is $10,000 enough for a bathroom remodel? — Enough for a cosmetic refresh (vanity, fixtures, tile, paint); a full gut remodel with layout changes typically runs $15,000-$30,000+.
5. How much does it cost to rip out and replace a bathroom? — A full gut-and-replace in a similar footprint typically runs $18,000-$35,000 depending on tile, fixtures, and whether plumbing moves.

## Home Additions
6. Is it cheaper to build a house or add an addition? — An addition is almost always cheaper per square foot than new construction since it shares an existing foundation, roofline, and utility connections in many cases.
7. How much does a 1,000 sq ft house addition cost? — Typically $150,000-$350,000+ depending on foundation type, finish level, and whether it's a second-story addition.
8. What is the average cost per square foot for a 20x20 home addition? — Most 20x20 (400 sq ft) single-story additions run $180-$400/sq ft fully finished, landing around $70,000-$160,000.
9. What is the most expensive part of an addition to a house? — Foundation work and HVAC/electrical tie-ins, followed by roofing to match the existing structure.
10. Is $100,000 enough to renovate a house? — Enough for a substantial whole-home renovation (kitchen + baths + flooring) on a mid-size tract home; a full addition-plus-renovation combo can exceed it.

## Garage Conversions & ADUs
11. Is it cheaper to convert a garage or to build an addition? — Garage conversions are typically 30-50% cheaper since the structure, foundation, and roof already exist.
12. What is the average cost of a garage conversion? — $15,000-$40,000 depending on whether you're adding plumbing/bathroom, insulation, and HVAC.
13. Do I need permission to convert garage to bedroom? — Yes — nearly every jurisdiction requires a building permit for a garage-to-living-space conversion, especially if adding plumbing or changing egress.
14. Is it legal to have a bathroom in the garage? — Yes, when permitted and inspected — it requires proper venting, water supply, and sewer/septic tie-in per local code.

## Foundation & Slab Repair
15. Is foundation repair worth the cost? — Yes — unaddressed foundation settling gets more expensive over time and can affect resale value and structural safety.
16. Does homeowners insurance cover foundation repair? — Rarely, unless the damage stems from a covered peril (burst pipe, storm); normal soil settling is typically excluded — this is exactly why an independent contractor, not a warranty claim, is usually the path once the builder's window has closed.
17. Can you live in a house while the foundation is being repaired? — In most cases yes, especially with pier-and-beam or slab pier methods; timeline is usually 1-3 days for typical residential jobs.
18. How much is a foundation for a 2,000 sq ft house? — New foundation construction runs $20,000-$50,000+; repair/stabilization of an existing slab is typically far less, $3,000-$15,000 depending on severity and number of piers.

## Whole-Home Renovation
19. What is the 30% rule for renovations? — A common budgeting rule of thumb: don't spend more than roughly 10-15% of the home's value on any single room, or more than ~30% total on a whole-home renovation relative to home value, to protect resale ROI.
20. What is the best time of year to remodel? — Fall and winter for interior work (contractor schedules open up after summer); spring/summer for exterior and addition work with foundation excavation.
